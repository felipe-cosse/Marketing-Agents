# AC-11 — Approved mock action survives interruption and retry

Status: verified by executable local public-API and worker acceptance checks.

Requirement: `Crash/retry after approval cannot repeat same mock action.`
Plan 06 distinguishes one semantic action/key and durable mock effect from the
number of transport invocations. It requires recovery after provider acceptance
but before local success, without reauthorizing or repeating that effect.

## Joined public-API and worker journey

`tests/acceptance/test_ac_11_mock_crash_recovery.py` uses the default runtime,
fresh migrated/seeded SQLite, real public Email-demo admission and approval
endpoints, real workers and original deterministic provider methods. Its two
parameterizations interrupt after the newsletter receipt or after the CRM receipt.
The latter also exercises recovery when the preceding sibling already succeeded.

Both exact action approvals are consumed and reserved by the real all-approvals
barrier before any connector call. The test records original action IDs, canonical
envelopes, hashes, keys, delivery contracts, plan and step IDs, requests, decisions,
uses and reservations. Public approval does not execute a connector inline.

A one-shot wrapper first invokes the real observed write gateway and retains its
actual return value. Only after the mock has committed its independent receipt
does the wrapper raise `asyncio.CancelledError`. The real worker propagates this
interruption without converting it into an ordinary classified connector error.
No proof, receipt, reservation, result, repository or successful response is forged.

At this exact boundary, the interrupted action is still `dispatching`, has its
original reservation, first unfinished attempt, lease and durable call markers,
and has no local success result. Its real receipt already exists and matches the
original action/hash/key/capability and observed result. An earlier sibling remains
succeeded; an uncalled sibling remains reserved with no attempt or receipt. There
is no model call or artifact yet, and all released approval authority is unchanged.

## Fenced reconstruction and completion

Closing and rebuilding the runtime retains those exact persisted facts. A fresh
connector ledger reports zero new inserts; it does not erase the old receipts.
The test derives its recovery instant from the later of the stored action lease
expiry and call deadline. At the call deadline, still before that action lease,
the real worker leaves selected business records and provider counts unchanged.

At the recovery fence, the real worker reconciles the interrupted action from its
original receipt without invoking that action again. If its sibling was uncalled,
that sibling executes once. The normal model step then creates one unsent Email
summary, verified through persisted provenance and the artifact API.

Final assertions join each action to its original request/decision/use/reservation,
observed proof and exact receipt, with two first successful dispatch attempts and
one successful model attempt/artifact. Old-ledger inserts plus reconstructed-ledger
inserts equal two. The plan, step identities, action envelopes/hashes/keys and all
consumed approval authority remain unchanged. Each original receipt row survives.

Audit checks require one `action.call_started` per action, exactly one
`action.receipt_reconciled` for the interrupted action, normal `action.succeeded`
for its sibling, correct receipt/attempt/final-version/state links, and both
`approval.consumed` events ordered before the first connector call.

## Terminal replay and explicit adapter retries

A second runtime reconstruction and idle worker pass preserve the exact completed
records and run audit history. Repeating the original public admission returns the
same work/run IDs and completed run; it must append exactly two original-work-linked
events, `ingress.manual_received` and `work.duplicate_returned`. Only the run's
timeline allocator advances by two. Every other selected business field and the
entire pre-existing audit prefix remain unchanged.

A separate adapter-level phase constructs a new genuine durable gateway and
repeats each captured dispatcher-created proof twice. It compares all four results
against the frozen original worker results and stored receipts. These deliberate
transport retries create no new effect, receipt, audit event or business mutation.
They are not new human approvals or permissions to dispatch another action.

The distinction is explicit:

| Phase | Observed gateway invocations | Total durable mock effects |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interrupted worker plus valid recovery | 2 | 2 |
| Four deliberate original-proof adapter retries | 6 total | 2 |

Subsequent worker passes remain idle, the model count stays one, and no external
connector read adapter is invoked. No duplicate effect does not mean every retry makes zero
transport calls; receipt-only worker recovery and direct adapter retry are tested
as separate contracts.

## Limits

This is simulated in-process cancellation and runtime reconstruction, not an
OS-process kill. The worker's normal cancellation cleanup releases its run claim;
the independently persisted action lease remains in force. Controlled time derives
recovery from durable deadlines and checks current lease/run-budget ordering; no
production delivery budget is widened. Broader crash windows and unknown-provider
outcomes are separate lower-level regression evidence, not additional default-
worker journeys. Some such fixtures use `create_all` or explicit policy/claim setup.

Snapshots reuse AC-10's selected business tables and add separately scoped run
audit checks. Session and worker-claim bookkeeping are excluded; the exact manual
replay timeline-counter increment is asserted rather than silently excluded.
All pytest gates are in-process with Python sockets denied except local Unix
sockets, without servers or child processes. Mock receipts do not establish real
email delivery or universal distributed exactly-once behavior. CI stays disabled.
