# SCHED-05 — Transactional next-occurrence persistence

SCHED-05 consumes one exact, already-committed, unexpired schedule claim in a single processing unit of work. The service derives the SCHED-04 policy plan from the persisted due instant and immutable claim time, obtains a write fence over every claim coordinate, and then persists the occurrence outcome, optional normal WorkItem and primary Run receipt, scheduler audit witnesses, `last_scheduled_at_utc`, the planned next UTC occurrence, schedule version increment, and complete lease release before one commit. The final schedule update is one integrity-sealed compare-and-swap over the claim version, due instant, recurrence version, owner, claim time, expiry, enabled state, and prior consistency digest.

On-time and `run_once` outcomes share the caller-owned SCHED-03 ingress path, so occurrence, WorkItem, Run, initial transition, and `run.received` audit never commit independently. A `run_once` occurrence retains its original due identity plus the exact bounded first/last/count misfire facts and admits exactly one receipt. A `skip` outcome persists one terminal occurrence with the same bounded facts while creating no WorkItem or Run. Every outcome advances from the recurrence plan—not processing time—to the first selected next occurrence, records `last_scheduled_at_utc` as the consumed due instant, clears the full lease, and increments the schedule version once.

Scheduler mutation events use the central globally ordered audit ledger without fabricating a Run for skipped work. The occurrence event is one of `schedule.occurrence_created`, `schedule.misfire_skipped`, or `schedule.misfire_run_once`; a second `schedule.next_occurrence_persisted` event witnesses the exact previous due, resulting last/next instants, disposition, and occurrence identity. These non-Run entries have an all-null Run timeline coordinate, restrictive schedule/occurrence links, event-specific redacted metadata, deterministic integrity fingerprints, and the same transaction as the schedule mutation.

File-backed SQLite fault injection proves that failures after receipt creation, after recurrence advancement, or at the sole commit leave only the separately committed original claim: no occurrence, WorkItem, Run, transition, processing audit, last-time update, next-time update, version increment, or lease release leaks. Stale, changed, and expired claims fail their exact fence or final compare-and-swap. PostgreSQL table DDL and constraints compile, but PostgreSQL runtime contention is not claimed. SCHED-06 retains expired-lease discovery, restart/reclaim races, and duplicate suppression after recovery; polling, metrics, APIs, authorization, migrations, deployment, and exactly-once Run execution also remain outside SCHED-05.
