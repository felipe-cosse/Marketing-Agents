# SCHED-02 — Only one worker claims an occurrence

SCHED-02 treats the schedule row's persisted `next_run_at_utc` as the due occurrence boundary and adds a short durable lease around it. A bounded ordered scan finds enabled due schedules, then a separate short transaction performs one conditional update bound to the exact schedule ID, expected version, expected due instant, current integrity digest, enabled state, due boundary, and lease availability. Only a successful update is ownership, and the service commits that lease before returning.

The lease stores its bounded owner token, claim time, expiry, and new schedule version. Equality at the expiry boundary remains active, matching Plan 08's strict `lease_expires_at_utc < claimed_at_utc` rule; a strictly expired lease can be replaced with a new version. Owner alone is not authority—the later processing fence is the schedule ID, due instant, owner, and claimed version tuple.

The integration suite synchronizes two independent services after both read the same due/version snapshot from file-backed SQLite. Exactly one CAS commits, the loser receives no claim, the due instant remains unchanged, version advances once, and no Work or Run is created. Restart, strict-expiry replacement, bounded ordering, future and disabled filtering, stale facts, rollback, malformed tuples, digest drift, and SQLite busy-snapshot handling are also covered without sleeps. SQLite and PostgreSQL DDL compile with portable completeness and expiry-order constraints.

This evidence proves exclusive active ownership, not occurrence creation or exactly-once processing. SCHED-03 owns durable occurrence identity and ingress, SCHED-04 owns misfires, SCHED-05 owns transactional processing, advancement, audit, and release, and SCHED-06 owns restart-safe duplicate recovery. PostgreSQL runtime contention, worker polling, audit and metrics, APIs, migrations, and deployment remain outside this requirement. Machine authority: [`SCHED-02.json`](SCHED-02.json).
